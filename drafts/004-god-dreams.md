God Dreams: Vision on Four Horizons

Summary: Will Mancini's insight that a church's vision has to focus at several distances at once — and what that has looked like as we have begun to work it through.

Of all the books commended to us during the course, the one that has changed how I plan is Will Mancini's *God Dreams*. Dr Thomas Lee generously and helpfully shared how his own church is working through the application of it, which was worth more than any summary.

The line I keep returning to is Mancini's account of what vision is actually for: to "help people go to places where they are given a larger story to live in" (Mancini 2016, 16–17).

That is a better description of the problem than most church planning documents manage. The problem is rarely that a congregation lacks goals. It is that the goals they have are too small to be worth belonging to.

## The eye adjusts; so should the plan

Mancini's central image is drawn from how sight works. Our eyes accommodate — they adjust focus for the foreground, the midground, the background and the horizon. A church's plans, visions and goals have to accommodate in the same way, adjusting to different time horizons.

[figure: figures/horizon-storyline.svg | Vision as horizon storyline. After Mancini 2016, 55.]

The four distances are not four versions of the same plan at different resolutions. They do different work. The ninety-day foreground is what a team can actually hold in mind. The one-year midground is where a ministry either takes shape or does not. The three-year background is where structural change becomes visible. And the beyond-the-horizon vision, at five to twenty years, is the thing that makes the other three worth the trouble.

The common failures are easy to name once you have the picture. A church that lives only in the foreground is busy and directionless. A church that lives only beyond the horizon has a beautiful statement on the wall and nothing happening on Tuesday.

## Our horizon storyline, so far

We are at the very early stages of adopting this, and a great deal is still to be worked out. This is where we have got to.

| When | What we are asking God for |
|---|---|
| December 2035 | 500 families — about 1% — within a ten-minute drive gathering with us |
| December 2028 | *still being worked through* |
| December 2026 | 50 families — about 0.1% — within a ten-minute drive reached; one new fellowship or congregation started; prepared to meet in the school hall |
| December 2025 | Welcoming team restructured · ministry to working-age men and women restarted · parents' dinner, preparing for a parenting course · four people coached |

Under God, three of the four ninety-day goals have been achieved.

I record that plainly rather than triumphantly. The value of the ninety-day row is not that it is easy to complete; it is that it is short enough to be honest about. A goal set for 2035 cannot be failed this year. A goal set for December can.

Our hope is that we will keep working towards seeing five hundred families gathered with us by 2035.

## What I have found hard about it

Two things, in case they are useful to anyone else beginning the same work.

The first is that the middle rows are the hardest to write, and it is not close. The ninety-day goals almost write themselves out of the diary. The 2035 dream writes itself out of prayer. The one- and three-year rows have to be *reasoned* — they are where you commit to the specific path by which the dream might actually arrive, and where being wrong is embarrassing rather than merely aspirational. Our 2028 row is still blank, and I do not think that is an accident.

The second is that a numerical goal in a bilingual church is never only a numerical goal. Five hundred families raises immediately: in which language? Gathered where? As one congregation or as several? The horizon storyline does not answer those questions, but it does not let you avoid them either, which is most of its value.

## A caution I want to keep in view

There is a danger in a framework this satisfying, and it is worth naming out loud.

Mancini's horizons are a tool for focusing a church's attention. They are not a promise about outcomes. It is the Lord who adds to the church those who are being saved, and no storyline obliges him to do it on our schedule or in our numbers. James is blunt with people who plan in years: "you don't even know what tomorrow will bring... Instead, you should say, 'If the Lord wills, we will live and do this or that'" (James 4:14–15).

So we hold the horizon storyline the way we hold any plan — seriously, because sloppiness is not humility, and lightly, because the church belongs to Christ and not to our planning.
